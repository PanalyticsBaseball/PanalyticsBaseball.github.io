## Executive Summary

Max Meyer improved his 2026 results while substantially changing how he delivered and shaped his arsenal. His average arm angle increased across every primary pitch, his release point moved higher and closer to the center line, and his sweeper usage expanded from 11.5% in 2025 to 30.0%. The sweeper's xwOBA improved from .358 to .258, while the slider produced +8.2 runs and remained his most valuable individual pitch.

The improvement was not uniform across the arsenal. The four-seam allowed a .378 xwOBA and produced -3.2 runs, while the sinker allowed a .339 xwOBA and produced -2.1 runs. Meyer's breaking balls protected the overall profile, but their advantage declined as hitters saw him again. Overall xwOBA increased from .270 in a hitter's First PA to .310 in the Second PA and .350 in the Third+ PA.

The evidence does not support a simple physical-fatigue explanation. Four-seam velocity, induced vertical break, extension, and arm angle remained broadly stable within starts. The more actionable explanation is a combination of fastball location, reduced deception across repeated exposure, and a two-strike plan that changes without consistently improving its finishing quality.

## 1. How Did the Delivery and Arsenal Change?

Meyer's arm angle increased by approximately three to seven degrees depending on the pitch. The release also moved higher and closer to the center line. These changes were accompanied by meaningful shape adjustments:

- Four-seam induced vertical break increased from 15.4 to 17.4 inches.
- Sinker induced vertical break increased from 5.3 to 8.9 inches.
- Sweeper velocity increased from 86.7 to 89.0 mph.
- Sweeper usage increased from 11.5% to 30.0%.
- Changeup velocity declined from 89.3 to 87.2 mph while its vertical break increased.

The redesigned arsenal gives Meyer two distinct breaking-ball shapes: a harder, tighter slider and a sweeper with substantially more glove-side movement and depth.

## 2. Did the New Release Improve Pitch Pairing?

Public release and movement data suggest that several pitch pairs became more complementary:

- Four-seam–slider release separation decreased from 0.85 to 0.62 inches while movement separation increased from 20.3 to 21.6 inches.
- Sinker–sweeper release separation decreased from 2.44 to 0.85 inches while movement separation increased from 27.8 to 29.5 inches.
- Four-seam–sweeper retained nearly 30 inches of movement separation, although the average release points remained approximately 3.26 inches apart.

The four-seam–slider and sinker–sweeper pairings therefore provide the strongest public-data tunneling proxies. This does not measure the complete ball flight or the hitter's recognition point, but it identifies combinations that begin similarly and finish differently.

## 3. What Changed Across Successive Hitter PAs?

For this report, First PA, Second PA, and Third+ PA refer to each hitter's successive plate appearances against Meyer within the same game.

The fastball was vulnerable before the Third+ PA. Its xwOBA increased from .330 in the First PA to .412 in the Second PA and .424 in the Third+ PA. The slider and sweeper were much more effective during the first two meetings, but their results deteriorated sharply in the Third+ PA:

- Slider xwOBA: .248, .255, and .393.
- Sweeper xwOBA: .208, .251, and .338.
- Four-seam xwOBA: .330, .412, and .424.

The slider carried the largest late-exposure increase. The pattern does not mean Meyer should automatically be removed before every Third+ PA. It supports a flexible decision rule based on lineup quality, score, fastball command, and whether the breaking balls are still finishing outside the heart of the zone.

## 4. Did Location Contribute to the Exposure Penalty?

The percentage of fastballs located in the heart of the zone increased from 12.7% in the First PA to 15.3% in the Second PA and 18.0% in the Third+ PA. Fastball Chase% simultaneously declined from 31.7% to 16.8% and 18.0%.

The overall fastball Zone% remained relatively stable, so the issue was not simply throwing too many strikes. The location quality changed: more fastballs entered damage areas while fewer pitches outside the zone induced swings.

Breaking-ball Heart% also increased from 10.8% to 13.3%, but Chase% remained close to 37% across the three PA groups. That makes the fastball location trend the clearer immediate development target.

## 5. How Should the Two-Strike Plan Evolve?

The sweeper remained Meyer's primary two-strike pitch, representing approximately half of his two-strike selections in the First PA and Second PA and 46% in the Third+ PA. The slider declined from 27% to 23%, while changeup usage increased from 5% to 13%.

The additional changeups provided a different velocity band, but they did not match the sweeper's finishing ability in the Third+ PA:

- Sweeper: 43.3% Whiff.
- Slider: 29.4% Whiff.
- Changeup: 18.2% Whiff.
- Four-seam: 6.3% Whiff.

The recommendation is not to eliminate the changeup. It should function as a selective speed-change and weak-contact option rather than directly replacing the sweeper as the primary put-away pitch. The more important sequencing adjustment is varying how Meyer arrives at the slider and sweeper. Breaking-to-breaking sequences represented approximately two-thirds of all sequences beginning with a breaking ball in every PA group.

## Baseball Operations Recommendation

Continue developing Meyer as a starter. The higher arm slot and expanded sweeper created a legitimate two-breaking-ball foundation, and the improved season-level results are supported by expected outcomes. His primary limitation is not a lack of major-league weapons; it is maintaining fastball location quality and preserving the deception of those weapons through repeated hitter exposure.

The four-seam should remain part of the arsenal, but its role should be more intentional. Prioritize locations above the center of the zone and avoid using it as a predictable in-zone bridge pitch during the Second PA and Third+ PA. The sinker can provide an alternative fastball path when its arm-side location supports the sweeper pairing.

With two strikes, retain the sweeper as the primary finisher. Use the slider when its tighter shape offers a meaningful contrast and introduce the changeup selectively by hitter handedness, swing path, and prior sequence. Avoid replacing high-quality sweeper opportunities with changeups solely to create variety.

For workload decisions, use a flexible Third+ PA trigger rather than a fixed pitch-count limit. Fastball Heart%, fastball Chase%, breaking-ball location, and current lineup quality should supplement pitch count and score when determining whether Meyer should continue.

### Development priorities

1. Reduce four-seam and sinker exposure in the heart of the zone after the First PA.
2. Preserve the four-seam–slider and sinker–sweeper release relationships.
3. Diversify the pitch preceding the slider and sweeper without sacrificing location quality.
4. Maintain the sweeper as the primary two-strike finisher.
5. Define the changeup's role by hitter handedness and intended outcome.
6. Monitor slider and sweeper xwOBA during the Third+ PA.

### Monitoring dashboard

## Methodology and Limitations

This analysis uses Baseball Savant pitch-level data from 2025 and the 2026 regular season through July 18. The 2025 reference contains 1,040 pitches and 285 completed plate appearances across 12 games. The primary 2026 sample contains 1,791 pitches and 464 completed plate appearances across 20 games.

Expected outcomes are reconstructed from public Statcast fields and assigned to the pitch ending the plate appearance. Release-point and movement separation provide a pitch-pairing proxy, not a measured tunnel at the hitter's decision point. PA splits are descriptive and are not adjusted for opponent quality, lineup construction, park, weather, health, or game situation. Third+ PA pitch-level samples are smaller and should not be treated as definitive true-talent estimates.

Public pitch-level data cannot identify the exact mechanical cause of an arm-slot change or diagnose fatigue. The conclusions should be paired with video, internal ball-tracking data, biomechanics, health information, and advance-scouting context before changing pitch design or workload.
