## Executive Summary

Louis Varland's development from a struggling starter into a high-impact reliever involved more than a role change. His arsenal evolved in two stages: the knuckle curve replaced much of the slider in 2025, and the changeup became a legitimate third weapon in 2026. At the same time, his four-seam velocity rose to 98.8 mph and the pitch generated +12.3 runs.

The 2026 improvement is supported by expected outcomes and contact quality. His strikeout rate reached 31.5%, xwOBA allowed fell to .239, and Barrel% declined to 3.7%. First-pitch strikes increased even though his overall Zone% fell, indicating a more deliberate plan: acquire strikes early, then expand the hitter's decision zone.

The primary monitoring issue is the knuckle curve. Varland retained his fastball velocity late in the season, but the curve became harder and flatter. Its Whiff%, xwOBA, and run value deteriorated, particularly against left-handed hitters. The evidence points to secondary-pitch shape and execution drift rather than broad physical fatigue.

## 1. What Changed After the Bullpen Transition?

Varland's season-level profile changed substantially:

- K% increased from 20.6% in 2024 to 25.3% in 2025 and 31.5% in 2026.
- Chase% increased from 23.4% to 31.7% and then 36.4%.
- xwOBA declined from .356 to .311 and then .239.
- Pitching run value improved from -16.4 to +3.7 and then +21.0.
- Average exit velocity allowed declined to 86.4 mph in 2026.

The role change likely helped his velocity and exposure pattern, but it does not explain the entire improvement. His pitch mix, two-strike selection, and contact suppression also changed.

## 2. Which Pitches Drove the Improvement?

The four-seam remained the foundation. Its average velocity increased from 96.3 mph in 2024 to 98.8 mph in 2026, while its xwOBA declined to .244 and run value improved to +12.3.

The changeup produced the largest functional improvement:

- Usage increased from 4.7% in 2025 to 18.0% in 2026.
- Chase% increased to 45.6%.
- Whiff% increased to 34.4%.
- xwOBA declined to .180.
- Run value improved to +6.8.

The slider's reduced role is supported by the evidence. It represented 27.7% of pitches in 2024 but only 8.1% in 2026. Although its 2026 expected results improved, it remained a less convincing swing-and-miss weapon than the changeup or curve.

## 3. How Did He Finish Hitters?

The changeup was Varland's most efficient two-strike pitch in 2026, despite being used less often than the four-seam or curve:

- Changeup: 29.2% PutAway, 36.0% Whiff, 62.5% Chase, and .132 xwOBA.
- Four-seam: 25.4% PutAway, 20.0% Whiff, 50.0% Chase, and .187 xwOBA.
- Knuckle curve: 18.0% PutAway, 28.9% Whiff, 38.6% Chase, and .165 xwOBA.
- Slider: 12.5% PutAway and .304 xwOBA.

The recommendation is not to replace the four-seam as his primary finisher. It is to create more two-strike changeup opportunities, especially against left-handed hitters, while preserving the elevated four-seam path.

## 4. Did Fatigue Explain the Late-Season Decline?

The conventional fatigue indicators do not show a broad loss of physical capacity. Four-seam velocity increased from 98.34 mph in the first third of the season to 99.27 mph in the final third. Release height stayed between 5.38 and 5.40 feet, and Whiff% remained stable.

The results nevertheless weakened late:

- xwOBA increased from .216 early and .218 in the middle phase to .282 late.
- HardHit% increased from 31.6% to 40.6%.
- Barrel% increased to 5.8%.
- Chase% declined from 39.0% in the middle phase to 36.4% late.

This is better described as pitch-shape and execution drift than loss of arm strength.

## 5. What Happened to the Knuckle Curve?

The curve became harder and flatter in the final third:

- Velocity increased from 87.2 to 88.3 mph.
- Induced vertical break changed from -7.9 to -5.7 inches, a loss of approximately 2.2 inches of depth.
- Whiff% fell from a middle-phase peak of 41.5% to 26.8%.
- xwOBA increased from .228 to .331.
- Pitching run value changed from +3.6 to -3.6.

Location remained concentrated near and below the lower portion of the strike zone, so the decline cannot be described as a simple failure to throw the pitch down. The shape of the pitch changed within a broadly similar location plan.

## 6. Was the Curve Decline the Same Against Both Sides?

No. The late-season cost was concentrated against left-handed hitters:

- Against LHH, curve xwOBA increased to .371 and run value fell to -1.2.
- Against RHH, curve xwOBA was .242, although run value also declined to -2.4.
- Chase against LHH remained strong at 45.2%, but Whiff% was only 28.6%.
- Against RHH, both Chase% and Whiff% declined to 30.2% and 23.8%.

The curve still encouraged left-handed hitters to expand, but its flatter shape reduced the quality of those swings. Against right-handed hitters, the more direct concern was declining swing-and-miss.

## Baseball Operations Recommendation

Retain Varland in a late-inning relief role. Preserve the high-velocity four-seam and treat the changeup as a core weapon rather than a show pitch, particularly against left-handed hitters and with two strikes.

The curve should remain his primary breaking ball, but the organization should monitor its velocity-depth relationship after every appearance. A harder curve is not automatically better if it loses the vertical separation that supports its swing-and-miss profile. Against left-handed hitters, the changeup should absorb some finishing opportunities when the curve's depth falls outside its target band.

The clearest full-count development priority is avoiding predictable in-zone fastballs. Varland allowed a .334 xwOBA in 3–2 counts. A competitive changeup or curve beginning near the lower edge can provide a non-fastball strike path without forcing the pitch into the heart of the zone.

### Monitoring priorities

1. Knuckle-curve induced vertical break and velocity.
2. Changeup movement and velocity separation from the four-seam.
3. Four-seam extension and top-of-zone location.
4. Rolling 10-appearance HardHit% and Barrel%.
5. Curve results against left-handed hitters.
6. Full-count pitch selection and location.

## Methodology and Limitations

This analysis uses Baseball Savant pitch-level data from the 2024, 2025, and 2026 regular seasons. The sample includes 905 pitches and 238 completed plate appearances in 2024, 1,070 pitches and 296 plate appearances in 2025, and 1,240 pitches and 308 plate appearances through September 25, 2026.

The 2026 fatigue and curve reviews divide 71 appearances into three chronological groups of 24, 24, and 23 games. These splits are descriptive and are not adjusted for opponent quality, leverage, health, weather, or park. xwOBA is reconstructed from public Statcast fields and assigned to the pitch ending the plate appearance. Small handedness and location samples can move expected outcomes and run value sharply.

Public pitch-level evidence cannot diagnose physical fatigue or identify a mechanical cause. The findings should be paired with video, internal ball-tracking, strength and recovery information, and biomechanical review before changing the pitch design or workload plan.
